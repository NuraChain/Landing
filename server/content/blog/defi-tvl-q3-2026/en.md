DeFi's total value locked rose from $69.2 billion on 30 June 2026 to $95.4 billion on 26 September, a gain of 37.9% in under three months, on DefiLlama's figures. After a first half in which the figure fell, it is a sharp reversal. It is also mostly a price move: ether rose about 67% over the same period, and a large share of what is locked in DeFi is ether.

## Key facts

- **DeFi TVL:** $69.18 billion to $95.42 billion between 30 June and 26 September 2026, according to DefiLlama.
- **The price effect:** ETH rose about 67% in the quarter.
- **Stablecoin supply:** $309.6 billion to $311.6 billion, up 0.7% — almost flat.
- **Trading:** decentralised exchange volume reached $721.7 billion for the quarter to 26 September, up 13.4% on the second quarter.
- **Largest protocols:** Lido at $26.4 billion and Aave V3 at $18.2 billion.

## What does TVL actually measure?

The dollar value of assets deposited in smart contracts, at today's prices.

That last clause does the damage. If a protocol holds one million ether and the price of ether doubles, its TVL doubles and nothing has happened. Nobody deposited anything. A rising TVL is evidence of new money only once you have taken the price move out.

## How much of the quarter was real?

Some, and less than the headline.

The cleanest test is stablecoins, because a dollar token does not appreciate. Their supply grew 0.7% in the quarter. If tens of billions of new dollars had arrived in DeFi, that number would have moved.

The second test is to count in the asset rather than in dollars. Lido's TVL rose 82% in dollar terms; measured in ether, liquid staking grew by somewhere between 7% and 17%. The analysis behind these figures puts it directly: it was "a price-led recovery with a smaller amount of new money on top."

Activity is the part that did grow. Exchange volume cannot be inflated by a price chart in the same way, and it rose 13.4% quarter on quarter, with September the strongest month since February.

## What changed underneath?

Collateral got broader. On 25 September, Aave V4 went live on Base with a market that accepts seven tokenized US stocks issued by Coinbase as collateral for USDC loans. It opened deliberately small — a $29 million cap on collateral and $21 million on borrowing — and isolated from Aave's other markets, so a problem there stays there.

It is a small market and a significant direction: lending against tokenized equities is the point where [tokenized assets](/blog/tokenized-treasuries-rwa-2026) stop being a wrapper and start being used.

## How should you read a TVL number on any chain?

- **Ask what it is denominated in.** A dollar figure moves with prices; a token count does not.
- **Check the stablecoin share.** It is the part that cannot be flattered by a rally.
- **Look for double counting.** A token deposited in one protocol, and its receipt deposited in another, can be counted twice.
- **Prefer what you can read yourself.** A contract's balance is public.

That last point is how Nura Chain's own figure works. The site counts bridged value from the supply of the bridged token contracts, which anyone can read from the chain — [how to read the Nura Chain explorer](/blog/how-to-use-nura-chain-explorer) shows where. It carries the same caveat as every such figure: supply tells you what was issued, priced today.

Figures are from [DefiLlama](https://defillama.com) as of the dates given, and other trackers count differently.
