On 19 October 2026 the crypto payments company Mesh announced a partnership with Visa that would let people pay in shops and online straight from the crypto and stablecoin balances in their wallet apps, with no separate crypto card. Mesh converts the balance at the moment of payment and the merchant receives an ordinary Visa payment, across what Visa counts as more than 175 million merchant locations. It is not live yet: no launch date, devices or launch wallets have been named. The same day Mastercard unveiled Stablecoin Security, which is a different thing — risk screening for banks that move stablecoins, not a way for shoppers to pay.

## Key facts

- **Who and when:** Mesh, in a release dated San Francisco, 19 October 2026, in which Visa's Global Head of Growth, Rubail Birwadker, is quoted.
- **What:** participating wallets and platforms could let users tap a contactless terminal or check out online, paid from balances they already hold. A Visa credential is provisioned behind the scenes.
- **Merchants:** no new hardware and no checkout changes; they receive a standard Visa payment.
- **Scale:** more than 175 million merchant locations in over 200 countries and territories, and $14.2 trillion in payments in fiscal 2025 — Visa's figures for its whole network, as given in Mesh's release. That is where Visa is accepted, not where this already works.
- **Not yet announced:** the launch date, supported devices and participating platforms; Mesh says these will follow as rollout details are finalised.
- **Mastercard, same day:** Stablecoin Security, unveiled at Money20/20 USA, screens stablecoin payments for fraud, scams and sanctions exposure before funds move onchain. North America comes first, with worldwide commercial availability expected in 2027.

## How would paying from a crypto wallet work?

The wallet carries a card it never shows you.

Today, spending a stablecoin balance usually means acting first. Mesh's release names the two usual routes: cash out to a bank account and wait, or sign up for a card tied to a single platform. Crypto card spending was about $1.5 billion a month by late 2025, according to American Banker as cited in the release — small next to the card networks.

The Mesh design moves the card inside the wallet. A participating app holds a Visa credential for you; when you tap or check out, Mesh works out how the payment is funded and converts across crypto, stablecoins and ordinary money, and the merchant is paid in the usual way. Mesh acts as program manager, so it handles onboarding and identity checks (KYC), and the platform does not have to run a card program of its own.

"Crypto never had a spending problem — it has a last-mile problem," said Bam Azizi, Mesh's chief executive and co-founder. Mesh says more than 200 enterprises already use its network, and names Kalshi, Tools for Humanity, Félix Pago, MAJORITY and KAST among its partners.

## Does the merchant accept crypto?

No. The merchant accepts Visa.

The conversion happens before the payment reaches the shop, so the checkout sees what it always sees. That is the point of the design — no merchant has to change anything — and it also means this is not crypto settling at the till. It is a crypto balance turned into a card payment at the last moment.

## What did Mastercard announce?

A tool for banks, not for shoppers.

Stablecoin Security is a risk-intelligence service delivered through an API. It looks at where the funds came from, exposure to restricted parties and signs of a scam, and returns a risk score before the payment is finalised. The first partners are Borderless.xyz and Keyrails, and the stated focus is cross-border and business-to-business payments. Mastercard cites a Bank for International Settlements study of 2025 Ethereum activity: of 593 million stablecoin transfer events, nearly 60% sat inside complex, multi-step transactions rather than simple one-to-one payments. Mastercard, which acquired the stablecoin infrastructure firm BVNK in August 2026, plans to extend the service through BVNK in 2027.

Read together, one network is making crypto balances spendable, and the other is building the checks banks will want before they move stablecoins at all.

## What should you check before using it?

When a wallet you use switches this on, four questions are worth asking.

- **Which balance pays?** Mesh decides how each payment is funded and converted; ask the app which asset is sold, and at what rate.
- **What does conversion cost?** The announcement states no fees.
- **Who knows who you are?** Mesh runs onboarding and KYC, so you will be identified. The release does not say which kinds of wallet qualify, or how a self-custody wallet would take part.
- **Where does it work?** No launch country has been named.
